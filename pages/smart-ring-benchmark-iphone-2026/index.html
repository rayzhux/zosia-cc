01

Bottom line

You asked for a fashionable, easy-to-use, health-focused ring benchmark for two iPhone users. That rules out a surprising amount of the market. The category looks crowded, but most of the real value is concentrated in three brands, with one clear legacy leader.

Best overall

Oura Ring 4

The best app, the strongest interpretation layer, the most polished women’s-health stack, and the most “this feels finished” experience. Downside: subscription, and it’s still not the slimmest ring.

Best complete package
Best value

RingConn Gen 2

Excellent battery life, no subscription, slim and jewelry-like, and iPhone-friendly. The app is less refined than Oura, but the hardware/value equation is frankly kind of brutal for competitors.

Best no-fee pick
Best ignored by iPhone users

Samsung Galaxy Ring

Looks good, works well inside Samsung’s ecosystem, but it’s Android-only. For you, it’s basically out before the race starts.

Not for iPhone
02

The field — what’s real, what’s noise

I separated the market into three buckets: serious contenders, niche-but-real products, and stuff I would not recommend putting real money on.

Serious contenders

Oura · RingConn · Ultrahuman · Samsung

These are the brands that repeatedly show up in serious reviews, official benchmarking, and long-term tester coverage.

Real but second tier

Amazfit Helio · Circular Ring 2 / Slim · Evie

Interesting on paper, but each has a real problem: ecosystem lock-in, buggy software, limited sizing, or company risk.

Not worth your time

Generic Amazon rings, payment rings, mood-ring vapor

Lots of cheap rebadged hardware. Fine for curiosity, terrible for a serious long-term health wearable.

04

Fashion, comfort, and “does this look like jewelry?”

This matters more than spec sheets admit. A ring you hate looking at becomes drawer tech.

Design ranking
  1. 1Oura Ring 4 — widest finish range, strongest premium feel, finally much less “sensor bump” energy.
  2. 2RingConn Gen 2 — the most discreet and closest to a normal ring once worn.
  3. 3Samsung Galaxy Ring — very clean industrial design, shame about the phone lock-in.
  4. 4Ultrahuman Ring Air — nice finishes, but visually reads slightly more masculine / sporty.
  5. 5Evie Ring — purposeful design, but more polarizing.
Comfort notes

RingConn Gen 2 is consistently described as one of the thinnest and easiest to forget you’re wearing.

  • Oura 4 improved comfort materially versus older Oura generations, but it’s still not the thinnest.
  • Ultrahuman is light, but can feel a bit more substantial visually.
  • Circular Slim wins on thinness, then loses it back on software trust.
Practical point: if Karen cares more about elegant daily wear, Oura and RingConn are the least risky style choices.

07

My actual recommendation

Not the hedged version — the real one.

If you want the clean answer

Buy Oura Ring 4 for both of you.

It’s the least likely to annoy you, the most likely to feel premium every day, and the strongest on iPhone. It also has the best chance of making the data useful instead of just plentiful.

Who should do this: if you want the category leader and don’t care about the subscription enough to make it ideological.

If you want the smart-money answer

Buy RingConn Gen 2 — at least for one of you.

If I were optimizing for cost, battery, elegance, and “don’t fleece me monthly,” RingConn is the sharpest option in the market right now.

Best experiment: buy one Oura 4 and one RingConn Gen 2 for a 30-day side-by-side. That gets you signal instead of speculation.

My favorite practical move: Oura for Karen, RingConn for you — unless Karen specifically hates subscriptions too, in which case two RingConns is a totally sane move.
